# SUGR Historical Post-Mortem

This document separates verified historical evidence, attributed testimony,
analysis, and revival-team responses.

It exists to prevent interpretation or opinion from being presented as
established historical fact on the official SUGR website.

Historical facts and evidence classifications are maintained in
`HISTORY.md`.

This post-mortem uses that historical record as its factual foundation and
adds analysis and revival responses where appropriate.

---

## Evidence Framework

Historical evidence classifications are defined in `HISTORY.md`.

This post-mortem uses those classifications and adds two interpretive
categories:

### ANALYSIS

Interpretation of what the available evidence may imply.

Analysis must not be presented as historical fact.

### REVIVAL RESPONSE

Actions or principles adopted by the revival team in response to lessons
identified through the historical record and analysis.

Revival responses describe the current team's decisions and should not be
rewritten as explanations of what historically caused the previous project
to decline.

---

# Chapter 1 — Rapid Early Growth

## Historical Snapshot

Contemporary reporting during November 2024 documented substantial early
activity around SUGR shortly after launch.

The historical snapshot recorded in `HISTORY.md` includes:

- 1,621 holder wallets
- approximately 396,019 ADA DEX liquidity
- $9.17 million lifetime trading volume
- 18,250 trades
- liquidity increasing from 75,662 ADA on November 13 to approximately
  396,019 ADA by November 20

These figures represent a November 2024 historical snapshot.

They are not current statistics.

### Public Treatment

The November 2024 figures may be used as supporting historical evidence
when relevant, but they do not need to appear in the primary website
narrative.

If published, they must always include their historical snapshot date and
source and must not be represented as current project statistics.

The broader narrative may describe this period as one in which attention
and activity arrived quickly.

# Chapter 2 — Legacy Expansion

This chapter documents historical project expansion.

It does not define the current SUGR product offering.

Sugar Factory and other legacy services should not be reintroduced into
the primary revival narrative merely because they are documented here.

## Verified Fact

Historical evidence confirms the existence of Sugar Factory staking.

The surviving Factory interface states that reward claims operated on
10-day intervals from January 8, 2025 through January 6, 2026.

On-chain records also demonstrate SUGR participation in Cardano DeFi
liquidity infrastructure.

## Under Verification

Detailed Factory economics remain under verification.

Do not publish specific redistribution percentages, withdrawal fees,
burn percentages, reward amounts or other Factory mechanics as canonical
facts until their historical sources have been sufficiently verified.

## Analysis

The previous project added increasingly complex infrastructure and token
mechanics after the initial launch.

Possible operational consequences include:

- additional hosting requirements
- software maintenance
- developer dependency
- greater operational complexity
- continuing infrastructure costs

These are analytical observations.

They do not establish that project expansion or Sugar Factory caused
SUGR's decline.

Do not state that the Factory caused SUGR to fail.

# Chapter 3 — Market Decline

## Verified Fact

Historical market data from independently reviewable third-party sources
establishes that SUGR lost the vast majority of its market value after its
initial launch period.

Exact all-time-high values and exact percentage decline differ between
historical market-data providers.

The exact magnitude therefore remains under verification.

### Public Treatment

Allowed:

"SUGR lost almost all of its market value during the decline."

Avoid publishing a precise all-time high or exact percentage loss until
historical price sources have been reconciled.

The decline may be communicated as part of the project's history without
assigning an unsupported single cause.

# Chapter 4 — Infrastructure Funding

## Attributed Testimony

During August 2026 revival discussions, the legacy Sugar Factory developer
stated that funding intended to maintain Factory infrastructure became
insufficient after significant depreciation in both SUGR and ADA.

The developer stated that the original SUGR allocation was expected to
support infrastructure for longer, but its declining value reduced the
available operating funds.

The developer also stated that he personally continued paying for hosting
of the public sugarcardano.io website after the original funding became
insufficient.

These statements are first-hand testimony.

They are not independently verified financial records.

If referenced publicly, these statements must remain explicitly attributed.

They must not be rewritten as independently established explanations for
the project's decline.

# Chapter 5 — Structural Fragility

## Analysis

The available evidence suggests several structural vulnerabilities may have
contributed to the project's difficulty sustaining its infrastructure and
activity:

- operating resources exposed to volatile crypto-asset prices
- centralized infrastructure dependencies
- dependence on individual developers or operators
- ongoing hosting and maintenance requirements
- increasing product complexity
- declining token value and market activity
- insufficient durable community growth

These are analytical observations derived from the available historical
record.

No single factor has been established as the sole cause of SUGR's decline.

### Public Treatment

Prefer restrained language such as:

"The old project became difficult to sustain."

Do not present unsupported causal conclusions such as:

"The Factory killed SUGR."

"The developers abandoned SUGR."

"The market killed SUGR."

"The treasury was stolen."

None of those claims are established by the current evidence.

The historical decline should be acknowledged without sensationalism,
accusation or unsupported blame.

# Chapter 6 — Legacy User Recovery

## Revival-Team Record

In 2026, the revival team identified remaining users who may still require
access to SUGR held through the legacy Sugar Factory system.

The revival team entered discussions with the legacy Factory developer
regarding temporary restoration of withdrawal functionality.

The quoted restoration cost was 200 USDM.

The revival team requested:

- withdrawal access for existing legacy participants
- no new deposits if technically possible
- a defined withdrawal period
- evidence or invoice for infrastructure expenditure

This process remains incomplete until the Factory has actually been
restored and withdrawal functionality has been independently tested.

Do not announce the legacy issue as resolved before verification.

This section records a revival-team response to a legacy-user issue.

It does not establish a legal liability or obligation of the revival team.

Legacy recovery activity should remain separate from the current SUGR
product offering.

# Chapter 7 — Revival Response

## Revival-Team Record

A community-led revival of the existing SUGR project began on
August 23, 2026.

The revival preserves the existing Cardano SUGR asset rather than creating
a replacement token.

The revival is deliberately reducing structural dependencies identified
through review of the previous project.

## Actions Completed

Completed actions include:

- original Cardano SUGR asset preserved as the canonical token
- canonical Policy ID and Asset ID documented
- legacy website source recovered
- legacy website archived through Git
- historical legacy release preserved
- official SugarCardano GitHub organization established
- public website repository established
- production website deployed from GitHub `main` through Cloudflare Pages
- static website architecture adopted
- minimal ongoing website infrastructure cost established
- development history placed under public version control
- historical evidence documentation established
- revival specification established

## Actions In Progress

Current work includes:

- treasury transparency and multisig documentation
- legacy-user withdrawal and recovery coordination
- community rebuilding
- historical-source enrichment
- correction of external market-data and project profiles where required

Completion should be claimed only after the relevant action has actually
been completed and, where appropriate, independently verified.

# Revival Principles

The revival should prefer:

**OPEN INFRASTRUCTURE**  
over private dependency

**SIMPLE / LOW-COST INFRASTRUCTURE**  
over unnecessary server infrastructure

**PUBLIC DOCUMENTATION**  
over undocumented decisions

**VERIFIABLE ON-CHAIN IDENTITY**  
over ticker or name recognition

**TRANSPARENCY**  
over promises

**COMMUNITY**  
over speculative mechanics

The objective is not to claim that every previous problem has been solved.

The objective is to make the revived project progressively easier to
verify, maintain and understand.

# Documentation Relationship

The public project documentation should maintain clear separation between
different types of information:

**`HISTORY.md`**  
Records historical evidence and its verification status.

**`POSTMORTEM.md`**  
Records analysis, interpretation and revival responses derived from the
historical record.

**`REVIVAL-SPEC.md`**  
Defines current project principles, website requirements and revival
direction.

**`treasury/`**  
Will maintain current public treasury identity, multisig policy and
on-chain verification records once the treasury documentation has been
finalized.

Historical evidence should not be altered to support a preferred narrative.

Analysis should not be presented as verified history.

Current project policy should not be retroactively presented as the policy
of the previous project.

Private signing material must never be included in public documentation.
